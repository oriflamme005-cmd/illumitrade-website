Best TradingView Indicator for Oil (WTI & Brent) in 2026

Guide · Updated November 2026 · 6 min read

Oil reacts to headlines — supply decisions, sanctions, conflict — and gaps through levels that held for weeks. You can't predict the headline, but you can see when the chart is coiled tight enough that any headline will move it hard.

The short version: For crude, focus on compression (oil's biggest moves come out of tight ranges), divergence at the extremes and zones. The ILT® suite printed the Diamond at the April 2026 oil top before a ~17 point drop, and flagged Brent hours before a +4% breakout in October — both posted publicly.

What makes an indicator good for oil

  • It must read the coil before the headline. When oil compresses, the next headline sets the direction and the move is large. Seeing the coil is what lets you be ready.
  • It must survive gaps. Oil gaps over weekends and on news. Zones and alerts work better than tight indicator crossovers that break on every gap.
  • It must mark exhaustion. Oil spikes on fear and often reverses fast; divergence at the high is the warning.
  • It must work on WTI, Brent and energy stocks. Many traders trade the commodity and the producers. One consistent read across both helps.

Free tools worth keeping

Free tools that help on oil: volume profile for the price areas crude respects, ATR for sizing stops to oil's volatility, and the RSI for divergence at the spikes.
